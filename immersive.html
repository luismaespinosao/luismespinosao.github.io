Immersive Environments for Autonomous Study

User-Centered Design of VR/MR Study Environments · UTM

A research-driven design process identifying characteristics for immersive environments that support focused autonomous study in university students. Using a mixed-methods UCD approach with 151 students, three conceptual proposals were developed — mixed reality emerged as the preferred solution.

01 — Context

The Problem

University students face significant difficulties concentrating during autonomous study sessions — distracted by digital devices, noise, inadequate spaces, and lack of structure. This is especially acute at UTM, where the student population is predominantly non-local, residing in shared spaces with limited privacy and sensory control.

Extended Reality (XR) technologies offer a promising approach to mitigating environmental interference by modifying spatial perception. However, no solutions specifically designed for autonomous study sessions existed in the literature — creating a clear design gap.

02 — Methodology

User-Centered Design Process

The project follows the Requirements Analysis and Conceptual Design phases of the UCD process, using a mixed-methods approach across three instruments.

Literature Review Exploratory Survey (n=9) Structured Questionnaire (n=151) Stratified Sampling Thematic Coding Focus Group (n=10) Descriptive Statistics Conceptual Design
01 — Explore

Exploratory Survey (n=9)

A mixed-format questionnaire validated in the local context the difficulties around self-regulation and study habitability reported in the literature. Identified key pain points: digital distractors, noise, shared spaces, and lack of structured routines.

02 — Survey

Structured Questionnaire (n=151)

Applied to 151 undergraduate students via Google Forms (Sept–Nov 2025), recruited through non-probabilistic stratified sampling by major and semester. Sections covered: housing context, study habits, recurring distractors, and technology expectations. Sample calculated at 99% confidence, 10% margin of error.

03 — Design

6 Functional Requirements → 3 Proposals

Design requirements derived from empirical findings, ensuring proposals respond to real student needs rather than arbitrary decisions. Three proposals were conceptualized: adapted physical environment (P1), hybrid VR with Google Cardboard (P2), and full Mixed Reality with Meta Quest 3 (P3).

04 — Evaluate

Focus Group (n=10)

Conducted at UsaLab · UTM with 10 students from diverse engineering, science, and business programs. Each proposal presented independently via conceptual sketches and simulation video. Individual vote with justification after blind evaluation.

P3 — Mixed Reality
↑ Preferred solution

Students who preferred Mixed Reality (P3) valued its visual support indicators and ability to manage distractions like noise and family interruptions — even in domestic settings. Participants primarily from computing, civil, industrial, and design engineering programs.

Students who chose Physical Environment (P1) had pre-established study routines and preferred environments compatible with analog materials (notes, exercises). P2 was unanimously discarded due to workflow fragmentation.

"Even having space at home, mixed reality could help manage distractions like noise and family interruptions."

— Focus Group Participant, computing engineering

A key finding: students estimated MR hardware costs at up to $1,160 USD — the actual market price is ~$290 USD. Even at inflated perceived cost, P3 was the most chosen option, indicating that adoption willingness is not purely cost-dependent. When informed of the real price, several P1 voters showed openness to P3.
