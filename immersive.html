Immersive Environments for Autonomous Study

A user-centered design research process identifying characteristics for immersive environments that support focused study in university students. Mixed-methods study with 151 students; Mixed Reality emerged as the preferred solution.

01 · Context

The problem

University students face significant difficulties concentrating during autonomous study — distracted by digital devices, noise, and inadequate shared spaces. At UTM, the student population is predominantly non-local, residing in shared spaces with limited privacy and sensory control.

XR technologies offer a promising approach by modifying spatial perception. However, no solutions specifically designed for autonomous study existed in the literature — creating a clear design gap that this thesis addresses.

01 · Explore

Exploratory Survey (n=9)

Validated in the local context the difficulties around self-regulation and study habitability. Identified key pain points: digital distractors, noise, shared spaces, and lack of structured routines.

02 · Survey

Structured Questionnaire (n=151)

Applied via Google Forms (Sept–Nov 2025) to students recruited through non-probabilistic stratified sampling by major and semester. Covering housing context, study habits, recurring distractors, and technology expectations. 99% confidence, 10% margin of error.

03 · Design

6 Requirements → 3 Proposals

Design requirements derived from empirical findings — ensuring proposals respond to real student needs. Three proposals: adapted physical environment (P1), hybrid VR with Google Cardboard (P2), and full Mixed Reality with Meta Quest 3 (P3).

04 · Evaluate

Focus Group (n=10)

Conducted at UsaLab · UTM with 10 students from diverse engineering, science, and business programs. Each proposal presented independently via sketches + simulation video. Individual vote with justification after blind evaluation.

↑ Preferred

Students who preferred Mixed Reality valued its visual support indicators and ability to manage distractions like noise and family interruptions — even in domestic settings.

P2 was unanimously discarded due to workflow fragmentation. Students estimated MR hardware at ~$1,160 USD — actual market price is ~$290 USD. Even at the inflated estimate, P3 was the top choice.
