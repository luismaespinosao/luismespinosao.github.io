Tíku

Emotional Technology for Rethinking Men's Role in Society

A mobile application connected to smart wristbands, designed to help young men in Oaxaca, México identify and manage their emotions — in a culturally sensitive, stigma-free space. The name Tíku means brother in Mixtec.

01 — Context

The Problem

In Oaxaca, México, cultural traditions reinforce traditional masculinities — where anger is often the only socially acceptable emotion for men, and emotional expression is deeply stigmatized. Young men face significant barriers to emotional literacy.

Men aged 15–29 have the highest suicide rate in México, with 16.2 cases per 100,000 inhabitants (INEGI, 2022). A survey in Mexico City revealed that 67% of men are unfamiliar with the concept of "new masculinities."

Existing digital mental health tools weren't designed with this context in mind — they felt clinical, stigmatizing, or simply irrelevant to young men in Oaxaca.

70% of global suicides are men
67% of Mexican men unfamiliar with "new masculinities"
43.8% of students believed technology could help with emotional management
02 — Methodology

Design Process

This project followed a Design Thinking methodology across five iterative phases, grounded in participatory research with the target community.

Contextual Inquiry Focus Group Persona Development Empathy Mapping Brainstorming Storyboarding Wizard of Oz Testing A/B Testing Usability Testing
01 — Empathize

Contextual Inquiry & Expert Consultation

Conducted a contextual study at a university in Huajuapan de León, Oaxaca, examining emotional literacy and new masculinities among local youth. Interviewed psychologists to understand emotional barriers specific to the cultural context.

Students reported difficulty identifying emotions, often avoiding emotional expression due to cultural norms. Psychologists highlighted emotions like fear, sadness, and anger as particularly challenging for men.

02 — Define

Focus Group & Problem Framing

A focus group with 8 engineering students aged 20–24 in the university's Usability Laboratory identified key cultural barriers and design preferences.

Key findings: users preferred non-human avatars (animals, machines, abstract figures) over human representations, especially male ones. They stressed the importance of culturally appropriate visual design.

03 — Ideate

Brainstorming & Storyboarding

Brainstorming sessions using mind maps generated and evaluated concepts based on feasibility, cultural relevance, and social impact. The storyboard technique was used to validate the concept with participants of varying technical backgrounds.

This led to Tíku: a mobile app connected to existing smart wristbands, with customizable avatars and interactive emotional missions.

04 — Prototype & Test

3 Iterations: Lo-Fi → Hi-Fi

Wizard of Oz tests with 2 students assessed conversation flow and avatar selection. A/B tests with 8 users evaluated color schemes, fonts, and avatars. Final usability testing with 5 users across 6 task scenarios.

Key iteration: lighter color palettes were chosen to avoid associations with sadness; dialogues were shortened and made more youthful; avatar designs refined based on user feedback.

03 — Findings

Key Research Insights

"Users felt more comfortable sharing emotions with women due to fear of judgment from men — and they strongly preferred non-human avatars (animals, robots, abstract figures) as emotional companions."

— Focus Group Finding, Usability Laboratory
Avatar Design

Users rejected human avatars, especially male ones. Robot and animal avatars created psychological distance that made emotional expression safer.

Color & Tone

Light, warm palettes were preferred over dark ones to avoid associations with sadness. Informal, peer-like language reduced stigma and increased engagement.

Gamification

Reward systems (unlockable avatar customizations) significantly increased motivation to engage with emotional exercises and return to the app.

04 — Results

Outcomes & Impact

100% task completion rate across all usability tests (38 activities)
65→82% user satisfaction improved across 3 prototype iterations
3 prototype iterations: lo-fi → mid-fi → hi-fi with continuous testing

The project aligns with four UN Sustainable Development Goals: Goal 3 (Good Health & Well-being), Goal 5 (Gender Equality), Goal 10 (Reduced Inequalities), and Goal 16 (Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions).

Tíku was selected as a finalist at HCI International 2025 in Gothenburg, Sweden — one of the most prestigious HCI conferences globally — competing against student teams from around the world.
