Selected Work

🏆 Finalist — HCI International 2025 · Sweden

Tíku

Emotional Technology for Rethinking Men's Role in Society

A human-centered design project addressing emotional literacy among young men in culturally sensitive contexts. Tíku pairs a mobile application with smart wristbands that integrate biofeedback and gamified emotional tracking — creating an experience that feels personal, not clinical.

The project navigated complex cultural dynamics around masculinity and emotional expression, requiring deep participatory research and iterative co-design sessions with the target community.

Participatory Design Co-Design Biofeedback Gamification Mobile App Wearables
📄 Published Research · DOI:10.47756 · 🥇 MEXHCI 2023

Lucero

Therapeutic Device for Child Support During Parental Separation

First Place winner at MEXHCI 2023. A therapeutic product co-designed with children aged 5–10 to support the grieving process during parental separation — integrating play therapy, voice interaction, and child-centered design.

How I Work

01 — Discover
Research

Contextual inquiry, semi-structured interviews, observational studies. Understanding the real problem before touching a wireframe.

Participatory & co-design sessions, design thinking workshops. Solutions built with users, not just for them.

Iteration

Experience prototyping, iterative lo-fi and hi-fi in Figma. Fast cycles, cheap failures, better outcomes.

Test

Heuristic evaluation, usability testing, task completion rate, pre/post evaluation. Measuring what actually changed.

About

I'm Luis Mario Espinosa, an interdisciplinary Design Engineer pursuing a Master's in Interactive Media with an HCI specialization at Universidad Tecnológica de la Mixteca in Oaxaca, México.

My work sits at the intersection of cognitive science, inclusive design, and spatial computing. I design systems that support human attention, emotional well-being, and accessibility — from immersive VR environments to therapeutic physical products.

I approach every project with the conviction that good design is invisible: it works because it fits how people actually think and feel, not because it looks good in a Dribbble post.
